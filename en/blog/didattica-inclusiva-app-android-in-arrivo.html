From book to app: I'm building an Android tool for special education teachers

Anyone who works in special education knows it: a large part of the job is rewriting. A history page becomes a text with short sentences, a science chapter becomes a concept map, a school trip becomes a social story. In my book “Artificial Intelligence for Inclusive Teaching” I collected the method and the prompts I use to do this with AI. For a few months now I have been turning them into an Android app.

The idea

Keeping the book open next to ChatGPT, finding the right prompt, adapting it to the student, pasting the text: it works, but it takes time. The app brings these steps together. You take a photo of the textbook page, choose the student's profile and the kind of material you need: simplified text, concept map, quiz or a social story with pictograms.

It is designed for teachers who work with students with specific learning disorders and special educational needs, including those who are not AI experts.

Privacy first

We are talking about minors and often about sensitive information, so I made precise choices here:

  • the student's profile stays on the phone, encrypted, with a pseudonym instead of the name;
  • the AI only receives the teaching information needed to adapt the text, never the name, class or observations;
  • before each request you see exactly what is being sent.

Where things stand

The app works and I am polishing it. Before publishing it on Google Play I will run a test with a small group of teachers, who will use it for two weeks with real materials and tell me what doesn't work. For now the app is in Italian, like the Italian school system it was built for.

In the coming weeks I will explain here and on social media how it works, step by step.
