The link is live: thank you, Daniele Pasini

A few days ago I wrote here about how I met Daniele Pasini and his Metodo MusicArte. Today that acquaintance is a real, live page on his site.

From a message to a page

On September 20 I wrote about reaching out to Daniele Pasini, a flutist and primary school teacher, creator of the Metodo MusicArte. He replied right away: the "Siti Amici" (friend sites) section of his site exists precisely to give space to projects he considers worth it.

That availability is now a real, published page: metodomusicarte.com/sitiamici-gianlucademontis-tech-coach. No official announcement, no press release: just a link, added because someone thought it was worth pointing out to his readers.

Why I'm writing this

Not because one link changes anything for a site's fortunes — that's not the point. I'm writing it because mutual visibility between people who work independently, without press offices or marketing budgets, still works exactly like this: one teacher recognizing another teacher's work and deciding to give it some space. It's worth telling, not just receiving quietly.

Thank you, Daniele

Thank you for the time, for the trust, and for giving space to a project that talks about school from a different angle than yours — AI, learning support, learning disabilities instead of music and drawing. If you're into music, drawing or alternative teaching methods in primary school, it's worth a look at the Metodo MusicArte.

Reciprocity between small independent projects doesn't need contracts: it just takes recognizing someone else's serious work and saying so, publicly.
