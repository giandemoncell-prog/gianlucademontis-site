Music, drawing and math in primary school: Daniele Pasini's Metodo MusicArte

Looking for someone in Italy who blends music and teaching the way I do, I found a Sardinian teacher who does it from a completely different angle: drawing before the musical note.

A method born in the classroom, not at a desk

For years I've been trying to make AI and inclusion talk to each other: automation, learning disabilities, the AI Act. Music, a different language altogether, enters my work mostly through Bachata Vibes Music. Looking for others in Italy who blend music and teaching, I found Daniele Pasini, a flutist trained at the Conservatory and a primary school teacher, creator of the Metodo MusicArte.

The core idea is easy to state and anything but trivial to apply: what if a musical score were, first of all, a drawing? Since 2016 Daniele has tested it in every class of his primary school, weaving together music, drawing and math into a single path — now published in five volumes (Edizioni WE).

Two different angles, the same question

His work and mine start from far-apart points — his from a staff drawn freehand in a primary classroom, mine often from a language model generating material for an individualized education plan — but they circle the same question: how do you make an abstract tool (a note, a rule, an algorithm) something a student can actually grasp? His blog, updated regularly, sometimes touches on compensatory tools for approaching music reading — the same vocabulary I use every day working with individualized plans, just applied to the flute instead of reading or arithmetic.

A direct contact, no detours

I wrote to him to point him to my work and ask whether it made sense to get to know each other — similar but not identical audiences, two teachers who put their name behind a method of their own, the same region (we're both in Sardinia, he's in Quartu Sant'Elena). He replied within an hour: "Works for me. The 'friend sites' section is meant to give space to sites that deserve it."

No formal agreement, no press-release partnership: just two professionals who recognize each other's serious work and decide to give each other visibility, one link and one logo at a time. The simplest kind of collaboration, and one of the least often written about.

Why I'm writing this

  • Inclusive teaching doesn't have a single language: for Daniele it's the drawing that comes before the note, for me it's often an algorithm generating a differentiated worksheet — both work
  • Useful collaborations don't always come from a plan: sometimes a well-targeted search and an honest message are enough
  • It's worth looking at what teachers who'd never cross paths through the usual channels are doing — primary school and AI consulting rarely share an audience, but they share the same problem

If you teach music, drawing, or are simply looking for concrete ways to make the abstract more accessible, it's worth a look at the Metodo MusicArte.

Two different ways of asking the same question — "does the person in front of me really get it?" — turn out to be closer than their respective fields would suggest.
