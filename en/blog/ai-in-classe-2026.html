5 Ways to Use AI in the Classroom in 2026

Artificial intelligence is no longer a future promise. It's already on our smartphones, in our search engines, in the apps we use every day. The question is no longer "will it reach schools?" — it already has. The real question is: how do we use it well?

In this guide I'll show you 5 practical applications I've seen actually work in Italian classrooms, with concrete examples and free or low-cost tools.

1. Generate Differentiated Exercises in Seconds

One of the most time-consuming tasks for a teacher is creating variants of the same exercise for students at different levels. AI can do this in 30 seconds.

How to do it with ChatGPT or Claude:

  1. Write the base exercise
  2. Add the prompt: "Create 3 variants of this exercise: one simplified for special-needs students, one standard, one advanced for gifted students"
  3. Review and adapt

Result: 3 variants in 30 seconds instead of 30 minutes.

2. Immersive Environments with AR and VR

Google Expeditions has been discontinued, but the alternatives are better. With an entry-level headset or even just a smartphone, students can visit the Colosseum, explore the solar system or see how a cell works from the inside.

Tools I use:

  • CoSpaces Edu: creates interactive 3D environments (has a free plan)
  • Merge Cube: 3D objects with a cardboard cube and a smartphone
  • Google Arts & Culture: free VR museum tours
In an hour of Roman history, 10 minutes of VR at the Colosseum increase content recall by 30-40% compared to a frontal explanation alone.

3. Personalized Feedback on Essays

Correcting 25 essays takes hours. AI doesn't replace your assessment, but it can give you a useful pre-screening and help students improve before final submission.

Workflow I use:

  1. Students upload their draft to a shared Google Doc
  2. An n8n workflow sends the text to the Claude API with the prompt: "Analyze this text from a middle school student. Point out 3 strengths and 3 areas for improvement, in simple language."
  3. Feedback reaches the student within 5 minutes
  4. The student revises, the teacher assesses the improved version

Estimated time saved: 40% of correction time.

4. Automatic Quiz and Test Creation

Google Forms + AI is an underrated combination. You can generate a complete quiz on any topic in 2 minutes.

How to do it:

  1. Use Claude or ChatGPT with the prompt: "Create a 10-question multiple-choice quiz on [TOPIC], for [GRADE LEVEL]. Include 4 options for each question and indicate the correct answer."
  2. Copy the questions into Google Forms
  3. Turn on automatic grading

Bonus: for retake quizzes, ask the AI to generate different variants of the same questions for each student.

5. An Assistant for Lesson Preparation

Before every lesson, I spend 10 minutes with Claude refining the structure. It doesn't replace me — it helps me see angles I hadn't considered.

A prompt that works well:

I'm preparing a 50-minute lesson on [TOPIC] for a [LEVEL] class. Students already know [PREREQUISITES] but struggle with [DIFFICULTY]. Suggest a structure with hands-on activities, including at least one moment of collaboration and one of individual reflection.

The AI proposes, I decide. The final lesson is always mine — but it's better.

Conclusion

AI in the classroom isn't a shortcut. It's a tool that amplifies what you already do well.

The biggest risk isn't using it too much — it's ignoring it while your students already use it outside school, often with no guidance.
